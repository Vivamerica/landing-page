3. Questions buyers abroad ask, answered

Can I buy property in Indaiatuba, Brazil, from abroad or as a foreigner?

Yes to both, for urban property such as the apartments and residential lots on this site. Living abroad is not an obstacle: the purchase contract can be signed electronically, as explained below. The restrictions that the Constitution and the two laws below place on foreign buyers concern rural land: the Constitution tells the law to limit the acquisition of rural property by foreigners (art. 190), Law 5.709 of 7 October 1971 governs that kind of purchase, and Law 6.634 of 2 May 1979 deals with transactions involving rural property within 150 km of the border. A lot in a subdivision is urban: Law 6.766/1979 only allows land to be subdivided for urban purposes, in urban, urban-expansion or specific-urbanisation zones (art. 3). What a foreign buyer or a foreign spouse needs: a CPF, which is mandatory for foreigners too (Receita Federal, page cited below); documents translated into Portuguese (Civil Code, art. 224); and, if married to the buyer, to be listed on the mortgage application, because Caixa registers the applicant and the spouse as buyers (Caixa's FAQ for new loans, read on 21 November 2026). Getting a loan is a separate question, and the bank answers it: a Caixa document of general loan conditions, with no date in its text (the file on the bank's site dates from 7 April 2022), asks a foreigner to hold a permanent visa in Brazil. Ask the bank what applies today before counting on a loan.

Do you handle a purchase by power of attorney?

A purchase in Brazil can be signed by an attorney-in-fact, and we tell you at which step one is needed. For the off-plan purchase contract it has not been needed: in the sales we closed with buyers abroad, the buyers signed themselves, electronically. A power of attorney (procuração) comes in when another person will sign for you at an in-person act, such as the bank's loan contract at the branch or the deed at the notary's office. The Brazilian Civil Code (Law 10.406 of 10 January 2002) sets two rules: the power of attorney must follow the form required for the act it covers (art. 657), so a public deed calls for a public power of attorney; and acts beyond ordinary administration, such as buying a property or giving it as security, require special and express powers written into it (art. 661, § 1). Before having one drawn up, ask whoever will receive it, whether bank, notary or developer, for the wording and the form they accept.

Do I need a CPF, the Brazilian taxpayer number? How do I get one from abroad?

Yes, you need one. Brazil's federal tax authority, the Receita Federal, requires a CPF (Cadastro de Pessoas Físicas) from any person, Brazilian or foreign, who does not live in Brazil and holds assets there that are subject to public registration, and real estate is the first item on its list (Receita Federal page "Emissão de formulário para pessoas físicas no exterior", which carries no date, read on 21 November 2026; the Consulate General of Brazil in Montreal repeats the requirement on a page updated on 25 June 2025). A Brazilian who lives abroad does not need to go to a consulate: you fill in the form on the Receita Federal website and email cpf.residente.exterior@rfb.gov.br the signed form, an identity document, your birth or marriage certificate and a photo of yourself holding the document. A foreigner who lives abroad applies in person at a Brazilian consulate, booking through the e-consular system. The service is free (gov.br service "Inscrever no CPF no exterior", page last modified on 15 December 2025).

How do I make a power of attorney from where I live?

There are three routes. The first is a Brazilian consulate, which has authority to perform notarial acts for Brazilians (Decree-Law 4.657/1942, the Law of Introduction to the Rules of Brazilian Law, art. 18). Each consulate publishes its own rules: the Consulate General in Lisbon, for example, serves only in person, by appointment through the e-consular system, requires the CPF for powers of attorney that deal with assets, and asks a foreign citizen for a valid Brazilian migration registration card, the RNM (page updated on 24 July 2025). The second is a notary in the country where you live: the document receives the Hague Convention apostille, in countries that are party to it (Decree 8.660 of 29 January 2016), is translated into Portuguese, and the translation is registered at a registry office in Brazil (Law 6.015/1973, art. 148). The third is an electronic public power of attorney, made by video call on the e-Notariado platform before the notary of the place where the grantor is domiciled or where the property is located (Provimento 149 of the National Council of Justice, the CNJ, of 30 August 2023, arts. 286 and 303, sole paragraph). The platform requires a digital certificate issued by a Brazilian notary; how to obtain yours while outside Brazil is a question for the notary's office. When one is needed, we help you organise the power of attorney: the wording the bank or the notary asks for and which of the three routes suits you. The document itself is still made by the consulate or the notary.

How do I pay if I have no Brazilian bank account?

The contract and the boleto are in Brazilian reais, and money coming from abroad enters Brazil through a foreign-exchange transaction. That transaction can only be carried out at an institution authorised by the Central Bank of Brazil, and the exchange rate is agreed between you and the institution (Law 14.286 of 29 December 2021, arts. 2 and 3). The buyer who lives in Thailand paid the boleto from an account held at a Brazilian bank; the same law provides for accounts in reais held by non-residents (art. 5, VIII), and each bank says whether and how it opens one. Each developer and each land developer has its own criteria for buyers who live abroad. The most common is to ask for an account at a bank in Brazil to pay the boletos. We confirm the criteria of the development you choose with the company before you sign. We do not suggest an exchange rate or a moment to send the money.

Can a buyer who lives abroad get a mortgage in Brazil? Will Caixa approve a loan in my name?

Caixa's home-loan pages carry no rule for buyers who live abroad, and nobody can promise approval before the bank's credit analysis. Caixa Econômica Federal is the federal government's savings bank. On its pages "Financiamento de imóveis" (home loans) and "Perguntas frequentes" (FAQ for new loans), read on 21 November 2026 (neither shows an update date), there is not one line about a buyer who lives outside Brazil. What they do say: the application asks for a document showing the CPF, proof of income, proof of address and proof of marital status; Caixa may ask for other documents; and the contract is signed at a branch, with all buyers and sellers present on the appointed day. To ask the bank itself, Caixa has a line for customers abroad, in Portuguese, 24 hours a day, on +55 61 3533 2000, and it accepts collect calls (page "Atendimento"). Other banks have their own rules, which are not covered here.

Can I prove my income with payslips and tax returns from the country where I live? Can a relative in Brazil join the application?

The bank decides, and Caixa's page does not say whether it accepts proof of income from abroad. Caixa asks for "proof of income" and says it may request other documents (FAQ for new loans, read on 21 November 2026); the page does not say whether it accepts a payslip or a tax return from another country. A document in a foreign language must be translated into Portuguese to have legal effect in Brazil (Civil Code, art. 224). Adding the income of a relative who lives in Brazil is possible: the same page says that combining incomes does not depend on the degree of kinship and that there is no limit on the number of applicants; for loans that use FGTS, it says the income must be family income. Whoever joins the income is registered as a buyer and signs the contract with you. Buying in a relative's name is a different thing: the owner and the debtor are then that person, not you.

What happens to the balance due at handover? Can I use my FGTS while living abroad?

It depends on each development's price list, and it is the point to settle before you sign. During construction you pay the deposit and the instalments to the developer; the balance is paid with a bank loan or with your own funds on the date set in the developer's price list, which may be the handover or earlier. Since the bank only decides at its credit analysis, read the item on the consequences of cancelling in the contract's summary table (Law 4.591/1964, art. 35-A, VI) with the possibility in mind that the loan is not approved. FGTS, the Brazilian workers' severance fund, can only be used to buy the worker's own home (Law 8.036 of 11 May 1990, art. 20, VII), and its conditions look at where the account holder lives and works. Under Caixa's FGTS manual for home purchases (version 035, in force since 2 December 2025), the property must be intended as the worker's own residence, which the worker declares under penalty of law (item 4.8.1.1), and must be in the municipality where the worker works or lives, a neighbouring municipality or the same metropolitan area (item 4.11.1). The manual has one case for people abroad: when one spouse lives abroad, the FGTS of both may go into a property in the municipality where the spouse who is in Brazil lives or works (item 4.3.4.2). Caixa's page "Utilização do FGTS", read on 21 November 2026, adds that the fund cannot be used for a property for relatives or other people.

Is there an alternative to a bank loan for buyers abroad?

For some residential lots there is: paying in instalments directly to the land developer, with no bank. This is not bank credit: it is the price split up by the land developer itself, with the interest and the inflation adjustment set in its price list. Two examples from the lists we hold: at Residencial Monte Carmelo, an open subdivision, a down payment from R$ 16,000 and the balance in up to 96 instalments for a residential lot (Dominium price list of November 2026); at Ville de Provence, a deposit of 10% and the balance in up to 96 instalments (Urban Square price list of November 2026). The interest rate and the adjustment index of each are on the development's page, in Portuguese. Each developer and each land developer has its own criteria for buyers who live abroad. The most common is to ask for an account at a bank in Brazil to pay the boletos. We confirm the criteria of the development you choose with the company before you sign.

Is an electronic signature valid in Brazil? Can the deed be signed from abroad?

Yes, it is valid. Medida Provisória 2.200-2 of 24 August 2001 accepts an electronic signature made outside the ICP-Brasil certification system when the parties accept it as valid (art. 10, § 2); this is what covers the off-plan purchase contract. The deed is a separate step. The law requires a public deed (escritura pública) to transfer real estate worth more than 30 minimum wages (Civil Code, art. 108), with exceptions: the bank's loan contract is a private instrument with the effect of a public deed (Law 4.380/1964, art. 61, § 5; Law 9.514/1997, art. 38), and in a subdivision the fully paid contract serves as title to register the lot in your name (Law 6.766/1979, art. 26, § 6). When there is a deed, it can be signed from abroad: by video call on the e-Notariado platform, before a notary of the district where the property is located (CNJ Provimento 149 of 30 August 2023, arts. 286 and 302), or by an attorney-in-fact. The property only passes into your name when the title is registered at the land registry (Civil Code, art. 1.245).

What taxes apply when a buyer who lives abroad purchases in Indaiatuba?

At purchase, the same as for a buyer who lives in Brazil: ITBI, registration and, when no bank is involved, the deed. ITBI is the municipal property transfer tax, and registration and the deed follow the notarial fee tables; the figures for Indaiatuba, with the municipal law and the 2026 tables, are in our guide ITBI, deed and registration costs in Indaiatuba (in Portuguese), and the owner's costs after handover are in what changes in your payments after the keys (in Portuguese). What changes for a buyer abroad is income tax. For a person who is not tax resident in Brazil, the Receita Federal taxes rent from a property in Brazil at source, at 15% when there is no double-taxation agreement with the country where the person lives, and taxes a sale on the capital gain, at rates from 15% to 22.5% and without the exemptions available to residents (Receita Federal, "Perguntas e Respostas IRPF 2026", version of 23 April 2026, questions 129 and 208). Whether you are tax resident in Brazil, and what that changes in your tax return, is a matter for an accountant.

Can I cancel after signing the contract at a distance?

The law gives 7 days to withdraw from a contract signed "at sales stands and away from the developer's head office"; it does not say whether a contract signed online falls under that rule. In the case the law describes, there is a right of withdrawal within a non-extendable period of 7 days, with a refund of everything paid, including the brokerage commission (Law 4.591/1964, art. 67-A, § 10, added by Law 13.786 of 27 December 2018). The buyer proves the deadline was met by registered letter with acknowledgement of receipt, and the posting date is what counts (§ 11). After the 7 days the contract is irrevocable (§ 12), and withdrawing follows the cancellation rules written in the summary table. For a lot, the summary table must state the right of withdrawal under the Consumer Protection Code in contracts signed at sales stands and away from the land developer's head office or place of business (Law 6.766/1979, art. 26-A, VII); the Code gives 7 days from the signature when the contract is made away from the place of business, "especially by telephone or at home" (Law 8.078/1990, art. 49). The summary table of an apartment contract must carry the same information (Law 4.591/1964, art. 35-A, VIII): before signing, check there how this right is written in your own contract.
